# Fruit Croquant — Information We Need From You, and How to Get It

Each item explains: what we need, why, and how to get it step by step.

---

## 1. PayPal Business account + connection key (Client ID)

**What it is and why:** A PayPal Business account lets customers pay by card or PayPal balance, with the money going to your account. A personal account cannot be connected to an online shop.

**How:**
1. Go to **business.paypal.com** → "Create account" → choose Business.
2. Register with your email and business details (trade name, address, activity: food sales).
3. Link your French bank account (the same one as your IBAN) so funds can be withdrawn to it.
4. **Connection key (Client ID):** a technical code that lets the website talk to your account — don't worry about the details:
   - Easiest way: give us the email and a temporary password (or add us as a secondary user in the settings) and we'll retrieve it in a few minutes, then you change the password.
   - Or yourself: go to **developer.paypal.com** with the same PayPal account → Apps & Credentials → **Live** tab → Create App → copy the **Client ID** and **Secret** and send them to us (through a secure channel, never posted publicly).

## 2. Bank account (IBAN + BIC)

**Why:** Shown to customers who choose to pay by bank transfer.

**How:** In France every account has a document called a **RIB** (Relevé d'Identité Bancaire), available in your banking app — usually a button labelled "RIB" or under "Mes documents". Take a screenshot or download the PDF and send it: it contains the IBAN, the BIC and the account holder's name — everything we need.

**+ A small decision:** How many days should we wait for the transfer to arrive before the order is cancelled automatically? (We suggest 7 days.)

## 3. Whish account (for selling in Lebanon)

**Why:** So customers in Lebanon can pay through the Whish app.

**How:** Let us know:
1. Do you have a business Whish account (whish for business) or a personal one? Business is better — request it from **whish.money** or at one of their branches in Lebanon (business registration documents required).
2. The number/ID customers will transfer to + the registered name (so the customer can confirm they are paying the right person).
3. **Which currency does the account receive?** US dollars or Lebanese pounds?

**+ Key decisions for Lebanon:**
- **Pricing**: Should Lebanon prices be shown in dollars (e.g. 30 g = $5), or stay in euros with the customer transferring the equivalent? We recommend dollars — much clearer for Lebanese customers.
- **Delivery inside Lebanon**: Who delivers (a company? a relative?), what does it cost, and how long does it take?
- **Stock**: Are goods shipped from France, or is there local stock in Lebanon? (This determines how we count available quantities per market.)

## 4. Legal information (mandatory for any shop in France)

**Why all of this?** French law requires every website that sells online to identify itself: who owns it, where it is based, and how a customer can reach them if there's a problem. This information is published on a page called **Mentions légales**. Missing it is an offence that can carry a heavy fine, and it also costs you customer trust.

### a. Legal form of the business

**What it is:** The status under which your business is registered in France:
- **Auto-entrepreneur / Micro-entreprise**: the simplest and most common for small, one-person businesses.
- **EURL / SASU**: a single-person company.
- **SARL / SAS**: a company with several partners.

**Where to find it:** On your business registration paperwork, or in your account at **autoentrepreneur.urssaf.fr** or **formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr**.

**If the business isn't registered yet:** Tell us right away — you legally cannot sell online on a regular basis without a registered business. Registering as an auto-entrepreneur is free, done online, and takes a few days.

### b. SIREN number

**What it is:** A **9-digit** number the French state assigns to your business — effectively its "ID number". (You may also come across the **SIRET**, which is 14 digits = SIREN + 5 digits identifying the location; if you send the SIRET we can extract what we need.)

**Easiest way to get it:**
1. Go to **annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr**
2. Type your name or your business name in the search box
3. Your business card appears — take a screenshot and send it; it usually contains everything we need (legal form + SIREN + registered address).

Official alternative: **avis-situation-sirene.insee.fr** (free PDF document).

### c. EU VAT number — or exemption from it

**What it is:** A number starting with **FR** followed by digits (e.g. FR12345678901), published in the Mentions légales.

**Important point:** Many auto-entrepreneurs don't collect VAT at all — a scheme called **franchise en base de TVA**: your turnover is below a certain threshold, so you add no VAT and your invoices must carry the wording *"TVA non applicable, art. 293 B du CGI"*.

**We need one of two answers:**
- "I'm under the franchise en base scheme — I don't collect VAT" → we put the legal wording on invoices, no number needed.
- "I'm subject to VAT and my number is FR..." → we publish the number and show VAT on invoices.

**Where to find the answer:** Your account on **impots.gouv.fr** (VAT section), or ask your accountant. If you're unsure, send us a screenshot of your account and we'll work it out.

### d. The address that will be published on the site

**Important warning:** The registered address appears **publicly to every visitor**. If your business is registered at your home, your home address will be on the internet. The common solution in France is **domiciliation** — a company rents you an official business address (~€20-30/month) used on your paperwork and website. Let us know what you decide.

We also need a **returns address** (can be the same or different).

### e. Contact details to be published publicly

An email address (e.g. contact@fruicroc.fr) and a phone number — the law requires a direct, responsive means of contact. You can use a dedicated work number if you'd rather not publish your personal one.

### f. Consumer mediator (médiateur de la consommation)

**What it is:** An independent, neutral body. If a customer has a dispute with you that you can't resolve between yourselves, they can turn to it **free of charge** before going to court. French law requires **every** online seller to subscribe to one and to name it, with a link, in the terms of sale.

**How:** An annual subscription of roughly **€60-100** with an approved body: **CM2C** (cm2c.net), **Medicys** (medicys.fr), or **AME Conso**. Registration is online and takes minutes.

**We need from you:** The name of the body you chose + its link. (If you like, we'll research the best fit and send you the registration link.)

## 5. Photos

- **Products**: one photo per product on a clean white/light background, **square** (equal width and height), in good daylight — a phone camera is perfectly fine. Comfortable minimum: 1000×1000 pixels.
- **Candy category**: one photo to represent it.
- **A photo of you** (at the market or in the kitchen): appears in the "Our story" section — portrait orientation preferred.
- Send them at full size without shrinking (we handle the compression) — via WeTransfer or Google Drive, not WhatsApp (WhatsApp degrades quality).

## 6. Product list

A simple table (Excel, or even a message) for each product: name in French, a two-line description, ingredients, category (fruits/vegetables/candy), and the quantity available in each weight. We'll handle the English and Arabic translations for you to review.

Also: are there any "local pickup only" products that aren't shipped?

## 7. Domain name (the website address)

A decision: **fruicroc.fr**, **fruicroc.com**, or something else? Cost is around €10-15 per year. We can buy it on your behalf and you cover the cost, or you buy it yourself from OVH/Namecheap and we connect it.
